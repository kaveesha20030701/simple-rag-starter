Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, is a technique that combines a search step with a language model generation step. Instead of relying only on what a language model memorized during training, RAG first retrieves relevant text from an external knowledge source, then gives that text to the model as context before it generates an answer. This helps reduce hallucination and lets the system answer questions about information the model was never trained on, such as private documents or recent events.

A typical RAG pipeline has four stages: ingestion, embedding, retrieval, and generation. Ingestion involves loading documents and splitting them into smaller chunks, because language models and embedding models both work better with shorter, focused pieces of text rather than entire long documents at once.
